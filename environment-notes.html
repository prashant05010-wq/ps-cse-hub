🌱 Environment & Sustainability

PS CSE HUB

1. What is Environment?

Environment means everything surrounding living organisms. It includes air, water, land, plants, animals and human beings.

Simple definition: Environment is the total surroundings in which organisms live.

2. Components of Environment

The environment can broadly be divided into two major components.

  • Biotic Components: Living organisms such as plants, animals and microorganisms.
  • Abiotic Components: Non-living factors such as air, water, soil, sunlight and temperature.

3. Ecosystem

An ecosystem is a functional unit where living organisms interact with one another and with their physical environment.

Main Components

  • Producers
  • Consumers
  • Decomposers
  • Air, water, soil and other physical factors

4. Natural Resources

Natural resources are materials and substances obtained from nature that are useful to humans and other organisms.

  • Water
  • Air
  • Soil
  • Forests
  • Minerals
  • Solar energy

5. Renewable and Non-Renewable Resources

Renewable Resources

Resources that can naturally replenish over time.

  • Solar energy
  • Wind energy
  • Hydropower
  • Biomass

Non-Renewable Resources

Resources that are limited and take a very long time to form.

  • Coal
  • Petroleum
  • Natural gas
  • Many mineral resources

6. Pollution

Pollution is the undesirable change in the physical, chemical or biological characteristics of the environment.

Major Types

  • Air Pollution
  • Water Pollution
  • Soil Pollution
  • Noise Pollution

7. Air Pollution

Air pollution occurs when harmful substances enter the atmosphere and reduce air quality.

Common Sources

  • Vehicle emissions
  • Industrial activities
  • Burning of fossil fuels
  • Burning of waste

Effects

  • Reduced air quality
  • Damage to ecosystems
  • Contribution to climate change
  • Respiratory problems

8. Water Pollution

Water pollution occurs when harmful substances contaminate rivers, lakes, groundwater or other water bodies.

Sources

  • Industrial waste
  • Sewage
  • Agricultural runoff
  • Plastic and other waste

9. Climate Change

Climate change refers to long-term changes in temperature and weather patterns.

Human activities, especially the burning of fossil fuels, have significantly increased greenhouse gas emissions.

Important: Reducing greenhouse-gas emissions and increasing resilience are important parts of addressing climate change.

10. Global Warming

Global warming is the long-term increase in Earth's average surface temperature, largely driven by increased greenhouse gas concentrations caused by human activities.

Major Greenhouse Gases

  • Carbon dioxide (CO₂)
  • Methane (CH₄)
  • Nitrous oxide (N₂O)

11. Sustainable Development

Sustainable development means meeting the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.

Three Main Dimensions

  • Environmental
  • Economic
  • Social

12. Waste Management

Waste management involves the collection, treatment, recovery and safe disposal of waste.

Important Practices

  • Reduce
  • Reuse
  • Recycle
  • Recover

13. Biodiversity

Biodiversity means the variety of living organisms, including diversity within species, between species and of ecosystems.

Importance

  • Maintains ecosystem balance
  • Supports food and agriculture
  • Provides useful biological resources
  • Supports ecosystem services

14. Conservation of Environment

Environmental conservation means protecting natural resources and ecosystems and using resources responsibly.

  • Plant and protect trees
  • Save water
  • Reduce unnecessary energy use
  • Minimize waste
  • Protect biodiversity
